# Voice Insights: the 4-minute EXCO speech (version 2)

**What is stronger than v1:** it opens on a cost, not a story. Every minute ends on a line an EXCO member can repeat in the corridor. Each part answers a question EXCO will silently ask: *what does it cost us, why not buy it, what is the risk, what do you want from us?* It ends on a clear ask.

**Format:** spoken live, demo video alongside. About 600 spoken words, so 4:00 with pauses. Time it aloud; cut from the bottom list if over.
**Rules:** one name, **Spark** (the voice and the team behind it). Say **suggestion**, never "recommendation". No number you cannot source.

## Timing and the line each minute must land

| Time | Part | Landing line |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:45 | 1. The cost nobody measures | "Our most valuable asset leaves the room, and we don't keep it." |
| 0:45–1:30 | 2. Debrief | "A transcript is words. A copilot is an answer. A debrief is a finished file." |
| 1:30–2:10 | 3. Product and campaign suggestions | "Every campaign we pay for finally meets the right customer." |
| 2:10–2:45 | 4. Proposal and advice pack | "Same day. Same shape. Every agent." |
| 2:45–3:30 | 5. Rehearsal | "Spark plays *this* customer. Not a textbook." |
| 3:30–4:00 | 6. Challenge Spark, and the ask | "The advice was always in the conversation. We just never listened." |

## 1. The cost nobody measures · 0:00–0:45

*(Walk to the centre. Wait for silence. Do not start until the room does.)*

> "Think about the most expensive hour in this company.
>
> It isn't in a boardroom. It's in a living room.
>
> An agent, a family, a home loan, a father getting older, a child starting school.
>
> Every one of our businesses depends on that hour.
>
> [pause]
>
> And then what do we do with it?
>
> We ask a tired agent to rebuild it from memory. That night. Notes. Fact-find. Record of advice. Follow-up.
>
> [pause]
>
> Three things go wrong. Detail is forgotten. A generic product is pushed, because the agent can't recall what we're running this month. And the next meeting starts cold.
>
> Our most valuable asset leaves the room. And we don't keep it.
>
> [pause]
>
> We built Voice Insights to keep it."

*(Start the video.)*

---

## 2. Debrief: not a recorder, not a copilot · 0:45–1:30

> "The meeting ends. The agent doesn't open a laptop. They talk.
>
> [pause]
>
> You've seen two things like this. A recorder gives you a transcript. A knowledge copilot gives you an answer, if the agent thinks to ask.
>
> Both leave the work with the agent.
>
> **Spark takes the work.**
>
> She listens the way Singapore speaks: English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil, even in one sentence. She says what she understood. Then she says what is **missing**. Five short questions. *Monthly or yearly? Is the father a dependant?*
>
> Twenty-six fact-find fields fill as the agent talks.
>
> [pause]
>
> A transcript is words. A copilot is an answer. **A debrief is a finished file.**
>
> And one rule we never broke: only a person can approve."

**WOW #1 · stand still:** "Ninety seconds. Nobody typed a word."

## 3. Suggestion agents: product and campaign · 1:30–2:10

> "One approval. And a team of specialists goes to work.
>
> Needs. Risk. Affordability. Product fit, from our own documents. Compliance.
>
> And the one marketing will care about: the **campaign agent**.
>
> Marketing uploads a poster. Spark reads it. An admin approves it.
>
> Then a customer says, *'I want to stay fit.'* He never says 'Vitality'. Spark understands what he meant, and puts that campaign in front of the agent, with his own words as the reason.
>
> [pause]
>
> Every campaign we pay for finally meets the right customer, in the right conversation.
>
> If nothing fits, it shows nothing. We don't push.
>
> And every line shows its source: the customer, our document, or the compliance check. If there is no source, it says so."

**WOW #2 · stand still:** "It doesn't only know our products. It knows what we are running today."

---

## 4. Proposal and the complete advice pack · 2:10–2:45

> "Then, the evening we started with.
>
> The proposal. And behind it, the complete advice pack: fact-find, record of advice, follow-up in the family's language, CRM note, brief for the next meeting.
>
> One conversation. Five documents. The agent reads, edits, signs. **Nothing leaves without them.**
>
> [pause]
>
> Here's what that means for this room.
>
> Compliance sees a complete file on the same day, in the same shape, from every agent. Risk is caught in the conversation, not weeks later in a file review.
>
> And for our agents? Their evenings back."

**WOW #3 · stand still:** "Same day. Same shape. Every agent."

---

## 5. Rehearsal: why it is not a coaching bot · 2:45–3:30

> "Now the next meeting.
>
> We all pay for coaching. Role-play. Scripts. Generic customers, generic objections, generic feedback.
>
> [pause]
>
> Spark plays **this** customer.
>
> She's built from the agent's own debrief: his budget, his worry about the loan, his father, the thing he hesitated on last time.
>
> She pushes back the way **he** would. Then she coaches the agent on what landed and what was missed, against **his** facts.
>
> [pause]
>
> No other coaching tool has this, because no other tool was in the room.
>
> Ours was."

**WOW #4 · stand still:** "A flight simulator for a conversation. And the passenger is real."

---

## 6. Challenge Spark, and the ask · 3:30–4:00

> "Last thing. And it's the one that earns trust.
>
> Don't believe Spark. **Challenge** her.
>
> Ask why a product came first. She answers from this family's file, with sources.
>
> Say, *'his wife is expecting.'* She re-runs the whole analysis on a copy, in half a minute. The real record is untouched.
>
> [pause]
>
> The agent can defend every suggestion, to the customer's face.
>
> Spark suggests. The advisor decides.
>
> [pause]
>
> It works today, on our own documents.
>
> So here is my ask. A small pilot, with a handful of agents, in two business units. We measure the time saved on **our** numbers. And Compliance reviews the controls with us before we go wider.
>
> [pause]
>
> The advice was always in the conversation.
>
> We just never listened.
>
> [pause]
>
> Until now."

*(Hold three seconds of silence before anyone speaks.)*

## Keep it honest (EXCO will test every claim)

- **Time saved:** use only the illustration from the 8-minute script (45 minutes a case, 8 cases a month, 6 hours per agent) and say the word "illustration". If you add it, put it at the end of Part 4.
- **"Two business units"** in the ask is a proposal, not a fact. Change it to whatever you want to commit to.
- **"No other coaching tool has this"** means rehearsal built from the agent's own debrief. If challenged, say that exact scope; don't claim to know every product on the market.
- **Languages:** say "understands", not "flawless". English and Mandarin are the best tested.
- **Campaigns:** marketing uploads, an admin approves. Never say "automatically live".
- If your final build has a button named "Challenge Spark", say its name at 3:30.

## If you run over, cut in this order

1. "If nothing fits, it shows nothing" (Part 3).
2. The second debrief question example (Part 2).
3. The "Compliance sees" paragraph (Part 4). Keep "Same day. Same shape. Every agent."

**Never cut:** "We don't keep it." · "A debrief is a finished file." · "Spark plays this customer." · "And the passenger is real." · the ask · "We just never listened."
