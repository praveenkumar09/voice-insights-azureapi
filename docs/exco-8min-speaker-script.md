# Voice Insights: EXCO presentation, 8-minute speaker script

**Audience:** AIA Singapore Executive Committee. **Slot:** 8 minutes. **Demo path:** Debrief with Juno only (no dictation mode, no live-with-customer mode, to save time).

**How to use it:** the **Say** blocks are written to be spoken, one breath per line. **Do** lines are what your hands do on screen. **WOW** marks the moment the room should react. Each 2-minute act has exactly one, so attention is reset four times.

---

## The shape of the 8 minutes

| Clock | Act | You do | WOW moment for the room |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–2:00 | **1. "Talk to it"** | Hook, then a debrief with Juno by voice | Juno talks back, names what is missing, and a meter fills as you answer |
| 2:00–4:00 | **2. "A team of eleven"** | Click Approve, agents run, the advice pack appears | Thirty seconds from conversation to a full suggestion and the paperwork |
| 4:00–6:00 | **3. "Challenge it"** | Talk to Juno, then a what-if: "his wife is expecting another" | One sentence changes the family, the sum is done by code, the analysis re-runs |
| 6:00–8:00 | **4. "Hands-free, and ready for the next meeting"** | Hey Juno, brief me, a taste of the rehearsal, close | You speak to the whole app without touching it, and Juno plays your customer |

Spoken text is about 1,000 words. The rest of the clock is the demo itself (Juno answers, the analysis runs), so **do not add words**; fill the waits with the lines written for them.

---

## Before you go on (not on the clock)

- **Sign in first**, in a fresh tab. Sessions were reset by the last deployment, so sign in again on the day.
- **Wear a headset.** Juno's own voice can leak into the microphone. A headset with a boom mic is the single best protection, in a room with applause or chatter.
- **Test the sound once**, in the same room, ten minutes before. Voice on, one short debrief.
- **Keep a second tab open** on the **Advice pack** of a finished case, as a safety net for Act 2.
- **Do not click "Download PDF" live.** It can freeze the browser. Show the tabs on screen instead.
- **Hey Juno mode: switch it on only at 6:00**, not before. Until then, use the mouse for buttons, so a noisy room cannot trigger anything.
- **Avoid the Admin screens** (Knowledge base, "Remove product"). They are reachable by voice and should not be near a live microphone.
- **Run the whole script twice** the day before, with a stopwatch, including the what-if.

### The dictation you will speak in Act 1 (about 25 seconds)

> "I met Mr Daniel Tan today. He is thirty-eight, a sales director. He's married to Mei Ling, and they have two children, Ethan who is seven and Chloe who is four. His father, seventy, lives with them and Daniel supports him. He's worried about the home loan and the children's education. He can put aside about five hundred dollars a month."

That gives a family of **a wife, two kids and a father**, so the dependants count is **3** before the what-if (children and parent) and becomes **4** after it. Check the count on screen in your rehearsal and adjust the last line of Act 3 if the number differs.

# ACT 1 · 0:00–2:00 · "Talk to it"

### 0:00–0:45 · The hook (no screen yet, or the home screen)

**Say**

> "Think about the last time one of our agents sat with a family.
>
> For an hour, they listened. They heard about a new baby on the way, a father who is getting older, a home loan that keeps them awake.
>
> And then the evening begins. Notes. Fact-find. Record of advice. Follow-up message. Typed from memory, late, and different for every agent.
>
> The conversation is the valuable part. The paperwork is where it gets lost.
>
> So we built a colleague who was in the room. Her name is Juno. Let me introduce you."

**Do:** open **Debrief with Juno**. Make sure the microphone is on and the language is English.

### 0:45–2:00 · The debrief

**Say, while you click the microphone**

> "This is what an agent does when the meeting ends. They don't type. They just talk."

**Do:** speak the dictation above, at a natural pace.

**Say (the moment you stop)**

> "Now watch what she does. She doesn't just write it down. She tells me what she understood, and then she asks me about what is *missing*."

**Do:** wait for Juno to read back and ask her first question (4–8 seconds). **Answer each in one short line.** Typical gaps: existing cover, the father's health, retirement plans. Answer with whatever is true to the story, for example *"He has a basic hospital plan, nothing else. His father has no cover."*

**Say while she is asking (fill the gap, one line per question)**

> "Up to five short questions. Only about what is genuinely missing. She never interrupts a pause."
>
> "And look at the meter. Twenty-six fact-find fields, filling up as I answer."

> **WOW #1 (say it, then pause and let it land):**
> "In ninety seconds, an agent has had a conversation with a colleague who read the whole meeting, and the fact-find is nearly complete. Nobody typed a word."

**Transition:** "Now the part I care about most. Is it just a transcript? No."

> *Not shown, but say in one line if asked: there are two more ways in, dictating after the meeting, and live listening during it. Today I'm showing the one that takes the least time.*

# ACT 2 · 2:00–4:00 · "A team of eleven"

### 2:00–2:40 · Approve and wait

**Do:** click **Approve**. (It needs a real click on the button, so use the mouse.)

**Say**

> "I approve it. And this is deliberate: **only a person can start the analysis.** The AI never kicks off by itself.
>
> Now, not one chatbot, but **eleven specialist agents**, one after the other. Needs. Risk. Affordability. Customer type. Product scoring. A check against our own product documents. And a compliance check.
>
> Every step is stored, so it can be audited afterwards."

**Say while the progress runs (about 30–40 seconds; stay on this and do not rush)**

> "This is the real thing, not a recording. About thirty to forty seconds.
>
> The old way, a good suggestion took days, because it waited for the paperwork. Here it takes about as long as a cup of tea being poured."

### 2:40–3:20 · The result

**Do:** open the finished result. Point to one product and tap a source chip.

**Say**

> "Look at the wording. It says **suggestion**, never recommendation. And it describes relevance in plain words, never a score. That is deliberate, in line with MAS guidance. The advisor decides.
>
> And every sentence carries a small chip. Tap one, and you see where it came from: what the customer said, the agents' analysis, or our own product document. A sentence with no source behind it is visibly marked. That is how this stays explainable to Compliance."

### 3:20–4:00 · The advice pack

**Do:** open the **Advice pack**. Click through **Fact-find**, **Follow-up message**, then stop.

**Say**

> "And here is the evening, done. The fact-find, filled in. The record of advice. A follow-up message to the customer, in their language. The CRM note. A brief for the next meeting.
>
> The advisor reviews and signs off. **Nothing is sent without them.**"

> **WOW #2:**
> "One conversation. Thirty seconds. A full suggestion with sources, and the paperwork drafted. That is the evening an agent gets back."

---

# ACT 3 · 4:00–6:00 · "Challenge it"

### 4:00–4:40 · Talk to Juno

**Say**

> "A good advisor doesn't trust a black box. So let's challenge it. Out loud."

**Do:** open **Talk to Juno**. Voice on. Ask, and wait for the full answer:

> *"Why did you put the first product first?"*

**Say while she is thinking (4–8 seconds)**

> "She is reading this family's file and the product documents. She won't invent anything. If it isn't in the file, she says so."

**Do:** once she answers, skip any further questions unless time allows.

### 4:40–6:00 · The what-if

**Say**

> "Now real life. Customers change their minds. And sometimes, the family changes."

**Do:** say to Juno:

> *"His wife is expecting another."*

**Say while she replies**

> "Look at that. She doesn't guess. She reads back the fact that changes: dependants go from three to four. Three plus one is four. And that sum was **not** done by the AI. It is done by code, and she shows the working. Numbers don't get invented."

**Do:** Juno offers to run it. Click **Run it**, or say *"Yes, go ahead"* (a clear, full "yes, go ahead").

**Say while it re-runs (about 20–40 seconds)**

> "She is re-running the real analysis on a scratch copy. The customer's actual record and the original suggestions are untouched. This is labelled as *explored*, not as real."

**When the comparison appears**

> "Original on one side, new on the other, product by product, with the reason each one moved, or didn't. If it hasn't moved, she says so honestly. Our demo catalogue is small, so you may see the same order."

> **WOW #3:**
> "One sentence from the agent, and a new baby is in the analysis within half a minute. The agent can *defend* the suggestion to the customer, and can test changes live at the table."

---

# ACT 4 · 6:00–8:00 · "Hands-free, and ready for the next meeting"

### 6:00–6:50 · Hey Juno

**Do:** switch on **Hey Juno mode** now. Look at the screen until it shows "Say Hey Juno".

**Say**

> "Everything so far, I did with a mouse and a mic. Now, no hands."

**Do:** say clearly, with a one-second pause after the name:

> *"Hey Juno, brief me."*

**Do:** let her talk for about 8–10 seconds, then say:

> *"Hey Juno, stop."*

**Say**

> "A ninety-second briefing on this customer, on demand. Or the full report, read aloud. In the car, between meetings. And she stops when I say stop.
>
> Any screen, any button. I don't touch the app at all."

> *If she mishears, say "Hey Juno" again, slowly. If the room is noisy, click the button; the point is the idea, not the trick.*

### 6:50–7:20 · The rehearsal

**Do:** say *"Hey Juno, rehearse"*, then *"Hey Juno, start the rehearsal"*. After Juno speaks as the customer, answer with **one line** as the agent. Then say *"Hey Juno, stop"*.

**Say**

> "And the last one is my favourite. Before the next meeting, the agent rehearses. Juno plays **this customer**, built from the agent's own debrief. She pushes back. She asks the awkward question. Afterwards, she coaches: what landed, and what was missed."

> **WOW #4 (only if the rehearsal runs smoothly; otherwise skip the last sentence):**
> "A flight simulator for an advisor's next conversation, where the passenger is the real customer."

### 7:20–8:00 · The business impact and the ask

**Say**

> "So what does this mean for AIA?
>
> For our **agents**: evenings back. Admin that was a long evening becomes a short review. Take a conservative illustration: forty-five minutes saved per case, eight cases a month. That is six hours back, per agent, every month.
>
> For our **customers**: same-day follow-up, in the language they are comfortable with. English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil.
>
> For **compliance**: risk caught in the conversation, not weeks later in a file review. And the evidence kept.
>
> For **leadership**: one picture, across the agency force.
>
> It is built for trust: ID and card numbers are never stored. Weak speech is filtered out. Juno suggests, and the advisor decides.
>
> It is working today, on our own documents. My ask: a **pilot with a small group of agents**, so we measure the time saved per case, and a **review of the controls with Compliance** before we widen it.
>
> And if you take one thing away, take this: the advice is in the conversation. We just never captured it.
>
> **Thank you.**"

## If you are running over

Cut in this order, and none of them breaks the story:

1. The second source-chip tap (Act 2).
2. The rehearsal (Act 4); end on Hey Juno and go straight to the impact.
3. Talk to Juno's first question (Act 3); go straight to the what-if.

**Never cut** the Approve, the wait while the agents run (that is your WOW #2), or the "three plus one is four" line.

## If something goes wrong on stage

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| Juno mishears you | Type the line in the box. The answer path is the same. |
| The analysis is slow | Stay on it. Say "that's the real analysis running." Then talk about what it is checking. |
| The what-if is slow | Same. Fill with the line "the real record is untouched." |
| Hey Juno does not respond | Say "Hey Juno" once more, slowly. If it fails, click the button and say "in a quiet room, no hands." |
| Juno cuts in or repeats | Stop. Say "let me ask that again." Use the typed box. |
| You are signed out | Sign back in. Switch to the second tab with the finished advice pack and carry on from Act 2. |
| No sound | Check **Voice on**. The answers also appear as text with sources. |
| Silence over 10 seconds | Say "she's checking the documents." Wait. If still nothing, type it. |

## Likely questions from EXCO, with honest answers

**Is this advice under MAS rules?**
No. It produces suggestions for the advisor to review, and the advisor decides. The wording, the sources and the audit trail were designed around that, but Compliance should review it formally before any customer-facing use.

**Can it make things up?**
It is limited to the customer's file and our product documents. Statements without a source are marked, and it says "that isn't in my notes" rather than guess. Sums are done by code, not by the AI. This is a pilot-grade control, not a finished one.

**Where does the data go?**
ID and card numbers are not stored. It runs on our own Azure OpenAI resource. Hosting, retention and approval of the services are decisions to take with IT security before the pilot.

**How fast is it?**
Juno answers about 4 to 8 seconds after a question. The full analysis takes 30 to 40 seconds, and a what-if about 20 to 40 seconds. What you saw was live, not a recording.

**What is the business value, in dollars?**
I haven't measured it yet, and I wouldn't quote a number today. The six hours per agent per month is an illustration built on 45 minutes saved per case and eight cases a month. The pilot is how we replace the illustration with AIA's own numbers.

**Why not use a general chatbot?**
It isn't tied to our customer file or our product documents, it shows no sources, and it doesn't produce our paperwork.

**What about noisy rooms and other languages?**
It handles English, Mandarin, Malay and Tamil, including mixed speech. A noisy room or a TV makes it less reliable, so use a quiet room and a headset. Spoken question-and-answer has been tested most in English and Mandarin.

**What does it cost to run?**
I'd rather not quote a figure from memory. The earlier pre-read has the cost assumptions; check them before you answer.

**What is missing?**
It is a working prototype for a pilot. The product catalogue in the demo is small, so what-ifs often keep the same order. A what-if never changes the record, and "make this the new suggestion" is not built.

## Numbers to double-check before you say them

- **45 minutes × 8 cases = 6 hours per agent per month.** This is an illustration from the pre-read, not a measurement. Say "illustration".
- **Do not quote** "+36% adviser premiums", "74% of critical illness cover missing" or "74,000 hours a month" unless you have confirmed them with AIA's own source. They appear in the product video, with the 74,000 hours labelled as an illustration, and I could not confirm the period for the 36%. This script leaves them out on purpose.
- **"Eleven agents", "26 fact-find fields", "30–40 seconds"** come from the working prototype and have been observed.
